# Moat analysis

Use after `quality-screen` has found returns above the cost of capital and the question is *why* they persist. A margin is a symptom. This skill wants the mechanism, because a return with no mechanism behind it is the one competition takes first.

## The five sources

Name exactly which of these the business has, with the evidence that proves it. "Several" is usually none.

| Source | What it is | What proves it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intangibles | A brand, patent, licence or concession competitors cannot copy | Price held above peers with volume held; a licence with a stated term |
| Switching costs | Leaving costs the customer money, time or risk | Retention or renewal rates; revenue per customer rising with tenure |
| Network effect | Each user makes the product worth more to the next | Share rising without price cuts; the second player shrinking |
| Cost advantage | A structurally lower cost per unit | Gross margin above peers *through* an input-cost spike, not only in a good year |
| Efficient scale | A market that supports one or two players | Entrants that came and left; capacity additions that were not built |

## Two tests the mechanism must pass

1. **Durability.** Has the return on capital survived one full cycle and one new entrant? A record shorter than a cycle proves timing, not a moat. Say how many years the evidence covers.
2. **Pricing power.** When costs rose, did price follow with volume held - or did volume fall, or margin? Only the first is pricing power. A company that "cannot raise prices because of the regulator" has a licence, not pricing power, and the two erode differently.

## What is not a moat in this market

- **A land bank.** It is inventory with legal risk, valued by `vn-valuation`'s real-estate method. It expires with the project.
- **First mover in a commodity.** Steel, fertiliser, seafood: the second plant costs the same as the first.
- **State ownership.** It can be a licence (a concession, a quota) or a liability (a mandated tariff); say which, and name the policy that could change it.
- **A good year.** One cycle of high returns in a cyclical sector is the cycle.

Moats that are real here, when the evidence shows them: a deposit franchise measured by CASA share and its stability through a rate cycle; a distribution network measured by points of sale a rival would take years to match; a concession or licence with a stated term and a renewal history; a consumer brand that held price through an input spike.

## Output

The moat source by name, its width - none, narrow, wide - with the years of evidence behind the call, and **one erosion signal**: the observable that would show the moat failing. That signal is written so a later run can check it - a share, a margin, a licence event, a competitor's capacity - and is the invalidator this thesis should carry. Never a price.
