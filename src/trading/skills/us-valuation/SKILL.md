# US valuation

Use when the debate needs a view on price against business. State the evidence in weights, ratios and trends, never as a level.

## Earnings yield and book-to-market

Earnings yield is the last year's net income divided by the company's market value. Book-to-market is shareholders' equity divided by market value. Higher means the market pays less for each dollar of profit or assets. Compare a name with its sector peers, not with the whole market: software and utilities sit on different scales, and a low number in a sector that is always low says nothing.

## Gross profitability

Gross profit divided by total assets measures how much the assets earn before overheads. It is a steadier read of quality than net margin because it ignores one-off charges and tax. Banks and insurers have no gross profit line, so the measure does not apply: say it is not available rather than calling it weak.

## Per-share figures bend

Buybacks shrink the share count and lift earnings per share without lifting profit. Stock pay does the reverse, quietly adding shares. When a per-share figure moves, check total profit and the share count before calling it growth.

## Missing accounts

Accounts come from SEC filings. A foreign company's come from its own annual report, converted to dollars at today's rate, so its growth and ratios hold while its older dollar figures move with the exchange rate. A name with no machine-readable filing, or no contact email set up for the filings feed, has no accounts here. Say the read is price-only; never treat missing as zero.
