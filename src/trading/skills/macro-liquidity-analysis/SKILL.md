# Macro liquidity analysis

Use when reading a macro regime or explaining why deployable capital is reduced.

## The asymmetry this product enforces

Macro input may only *reduce* deployable capital. It never raises a cap and never turns a hold into a buy. A benign regime removes a brake; it is not an accelerator. This is deliberate: macro calls are low-frequency and poorly calibrated, and a macro-driven size increase compounds a forecasting error into a position.

## What to read

- **Policy rate and its direction.** The direction matters more than the level for asset prices.
- **Real rates.** Nominal minus expected inflation. This is the discount rate everything else prices against.
- **Credit conditions.** Spreads and lending growth say whether policy is actually transmitting.
- **Currency.** A depreciating local currency imports inflation and constrains the central bank.
- **Cross-border flow.** Sustained foreign selling is a liquidity fact regardless of local fundamentals.

## Regime, not forecast

Classify the current state and say what it permits. Do not predict the next move; a regime call that depends on a rate decision is a bet on a committee, and it belongs in caveats rather than in a thesis.

## Applying it

- Risk-off regime: the deployable fraction is already cut; prefer names that survive a funding squeeze, and never ask for a wider risk-reward, which the levels set by rule.
- Neutral: no adjustment. Say so plainly rather than manufacturing a lean.
- Risk-on: remove the reduction, do not exceed policy.

## Output

Name the regime, the two indicators that place it there, and the specific reduction it implies - or state explicitly that it implies none.
