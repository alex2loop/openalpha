# US execution

Use after the plan exists and the question is how to place it. This skill never sets a level; it explains how the plan meets the market.

## Place the entry as a limit order

A limit order at the planned entry buys at that price or better. A market order buys at whatever the book offers, which on a thin name or a fast open can sit well above the plan. If the price runs away before the order fills, the plan has not changed: skip the trade rather than chase it.

## Avoid the first and last minutes

The first minutes after the 09:30 open carry the overnight news and the widest spreads. The last minutes before 16:00 carry index funds and large orders closing out the day. Prices there say little about the day, so a limit placed mid-session usually fills with less slippage.

## Reading a gap through a stop

A stop is a plan, not a guarantee. When a name opens below the stop, the order fills at the open. Read the gap before reacting: earnings and guidance explain most of them, and the thesis may be intact or broken. Decide from the reason, not from the pain.

## After the fill

Record the real fill price and size, since levels and risk are read from the book. Move the stop only along the plan's trail; never to entry.
