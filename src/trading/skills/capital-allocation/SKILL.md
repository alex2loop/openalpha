# Capital allocation

Use when a thesis leans on management, on growth, or on a corporate action. A business is what its managers do with the cash it makes; this skill reads five years of those decisions and scores them against the owner's interest, which is the only interest the operator holds.

## The five tests

1. **Does reinvested capital earn its keep?** Incremental return on capital over three to five years: the change in operating profit after tax over the change in invested capital. Growth funded at a return below the operator's required return destroyed value however fast revenue grew. Cite the two balance sheets and the two income statements the change is read from.
2. **Who paid for the growth?** Read shares outstanding and cash raised from share issues per year from the ANNUAL ACCOUNTS evidence. The share count over five years, split by cause:
   - a bonus issue or stock dividend moves nothing between owners;
   - an ESOP at par, or a private placement at a discount, transfers value from owners to the recipients - size it as the discount times the shares issued;
   - a rights issue to fund a project is a decision to judge by test 1;
   - a rights issue to fund losses or repay debt is the warning, and it is the commonest one here.
3. **Are buybacks and issuance on the right side of value?** A repurchase below the business's value helps the remaining owners; above it, it harms them. Issuance is the mirror. Judge each against `intrinsic-value`'s range, not against the price at the time.
4. **Is the dividend a policy or an event?** `vn-dividend-analysis` owns the cover and the dates; this test asks only whether payments follow a stated policy the board has kept, or arrive after asset sales and vanish after them.
5. **Is management candid?** Did last year's stated targets get met, and does this year's annual report say so where they did not? Related-party revenue and receivables, and whether they grew faster than the rest. A change of auditor, or a qualified opinion, without an explanation in the report.

## Structures that change the reading

- **State ownership.** A divestment timetable is a catalyst and a governance change at once; a state holder that appoints management answers to policy, and test 5 is read against policy targets rather than owner ones.
- **Holding companies.** The listed entity may not control subsidiary cash. Tests 1 and 4 are read at the level that holds the cash, and the report says which.
- **Pledged insider shares.** Not an allocation decision, but it converts a price fall into forced selling; `vn-deep-dive` carries it, and it is repeated here because a manager whose shares are pledged has an incentive the owners do not.

## Output

A pass or fail per test with the number that decided it, cited to the filing and period, then the one allocation decision - a placement, a project, a payout - that would change the verdict if it went the other way.
