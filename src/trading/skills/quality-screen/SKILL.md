# Quality screen

Use before a multi-month thesis, and whenever a name screens well on value.

## The five questions

1. **Does it earn more than its cost of capital, repeatedly?** One good year is a cycle, not quality. Look across a full cycle where the history exists. ROIC per year is in the ANNUAL ACCOUNTS evidence (in chat, `market_accounts`).
2. **Does profit become cash?** Compare cumulative operating cash flow to cumulative net income over three to five years. A persistent shortfall is the finding, whatever the margin looks like. Its trend line prints this as operating cash flow over profit.
3. **Is the balance sheet survivable?** Net debt to EBITDA, the maturity wall against available liquidity, and whether debt currency matches revenue currency. Net debt/EBITDA per year is there too.
4. **Is growth funded by owners or by the business?** Track share count. Growth funded by continuous issuance is not compounding for the existing holder. `capital-allocation` splits the count by cause and prices each issue. Cash raised from share issues and shares outstanding per year are there; shares rising with no cash raised is a bonus issue or stock dividend.
5. **Do the numbers move for one repeatable reason?** Revaluation gains, asset sales, and one-off subsidies are real cash and are not repeatable. Strip them before judging the trend.

## VN-specific quality flags

- Related-party revenue or receivables concentrated in an affiliate.
- Pledged shares by insiders, which convert a price fall into forced selling.
- Receivables and inventory growing materially faster than revenue.
- Repeated auditor qualification or a change of auditor without explanation.
- A holding-company structure where the listed entity does not control the cash.

## What quality does not tell you

Nothing about price. A high-quality business at any multiple is not a suggestion; pair this screen with valuation before concluding.

## Output

A pass or fail per question with the number that decided it, then one sentence on what would change the verdict.
