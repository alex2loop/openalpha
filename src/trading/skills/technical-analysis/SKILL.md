# Technical analysis

Use to place levels and judge timing for an idea that already has a fundamental or factor case. A technical pattern is not a reason to own a business.

## What this product uses price structure for

- **Trend context.** Is price above or below its longer average, and is that average rising or falling? This is regime, not a signal.
- **Support and resistance.** Prior swing highs and lows, and volume-heavy price areas. These are where a stop has meaning, because they are where the thesis is wrong.
- **Volatility.** Recent true range sets how far a stop must sit to survive noise. A stop inside the noise band is a donation.
- **Volume confirmation.** A move on thin volume is a lower-quality signal than the same move on heavy volume.

## Level placement

1. Place the stop where the setup is invalidated, not at a round number and not at a comfortable loss.
2. Derive the target from structure - the next resistance area or a measured move - not from a desired return.
3. Never ask for a tighter stop to improve the risk-reward. The market sets how far out the take-profit and target sit by rule, so their distance says nothing about the trade and is no reason to wait.

The deterministic pipeline computes and validates the actual levels. Your job is to say whether the structure supports them.

## Discipline

- Indicators are transformations of price. Stacking five of them is one signal counted five times.
- A pattern named after the fact is hindsight. Name it before the confirmation, or do not name it.
- Gaps, splits, and rights issues distort a chart. The market's own playbook says whether its prices are already adjusted; where it says nothing, do not read a level across one.

## Output

Trend context in one clause, the invalidation level with the reason it invalidates, and whether structure supports the proposed levels.
