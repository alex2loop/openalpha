# Crypto analysis

Use for any crypto instrument. Equity valuation does not transfer: there are no earnings, and cash flow claims are usually protocol revenue with no right attached.

## What carries information

- **Global liquidity.** Crypto is a long-duration liquidity asset. Real rates and dollar strength dominate most multi-week moves; a token-specific story rarely survives a liquidity tightening.
- **Funding rate.** Persistently positive funding means longs are paying to stay long - crowded, and vulnerable to a cascade. Deeply negative funding after a fall is capitulation, not a signal on its own.
- **Retail positioning.** The global long/short account ratio is the crowd's lean. Well above one after a rise means the ordinary account is already long and has less left to buy; well below one after a fall is the same crowd already out. It scores contrarian beside funding, and like funding it is a condition, not a trigger.
- **Supply overhang.** Unlocks, treasury sales and miner selling move price, but nothing here feeds an unlock calendar. Name one only when a headline in the evidence gives its date and size; otherwise say the supply side is unknown rather than recalling a schedule from memory.
- **Fear and greed.** Useful only at extremes, and only as a contrarian input to conviction - never as a factor.

## What does not

- Total value locked, active addresses, and developer counts are gameable and lag price.
- "Institutional adoption" without a named, dated flow is a narrative.
- Comparisons to another token's market cap assume the comparison is fair. It usually is not.

## Risk mechanics that are specific to this asset class

- 24/7 trading means a stop can be hit while the operator sleeps and no session boundary protects them. Size accordingly.
- Exchange and custody risk is a real loss path with no equivalent in a brokerage account.
- Drawdowns of 70-80% have occurred repeatedly within a cycle. A position sized for equity volatility is mis-sized here.

## Trigger and invalidation

The trend factor's metrics carry three prices. Use them, never a level of your own:

- **Trailing high (20 sessions)** is the trigger, and a close above it is the breakout. Relative volume grades it: at or above 2 is a confirmed breakout; below 2 is a weaker one that argues for lower conviction, never for waiting. Waiting a session for volume only buys a higher entry.
- **Trailing low (10 sessions before the latest)** is the invalidation. A close below it is the breakout failing, and the pipeline itself turns that into an exit on a held name; on a candidate, say it as the condition that voids the idea.
- A name within a few percent of its trailing high with the volatility factor abstaining is *at* the trigger, not past it. State the trigger as the entry condition rather than arguing the name is extended.

Breakout entries win 30-45% of the time and pay through the size of the winners. Say that, so three stopped-out trades are not read as the model being wrong.

## Output

Say which of liquidity, funding, or supply drives the current setup, and name the dated fact that supports it. If the only support is price action, say so - that is a technical case, not a crypto case.
