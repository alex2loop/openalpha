# Inversion

Use when the question is "how is this wrong" rather than "is this right". Start from the other side's best case, not from a list of things that can go wrong in general: a risk that applies to every name is not a finding about this one.

## The method

1. **Take the load-bearing claims.** From the case being inverted, pick the two or three claims that, if false, end the thesis. Ignore the rest; a supporting claim that fails leaves the thesis standing.
2. **State the failure.** For each, write what would have to be true for the claim to be wrong. Not "the margin could fall" but "the margin falls because the input contract reprices in the fourth quarter". Name the mechanism.
3. **Name the observable.** For each failure, the one thing a later reader could check that would show it happening: a filing line, a rate, a share, a licence event, a competitor's capacity, an unlock date, a funding rate. If nothing observable would show it, the failure is a fear, and it is dropped.
4. **Rank by damage, not by likelihood.** The failure that ends the thesis outranks the one that dents it, whatever their odds.

## What an invalidator is

The observable from step 3, written so a run six months from now can answer yes or no from evidence:

- A state or a number crossing a stated level, dated where the date is known.
- Never a price move. The stop already covers price, and "the price falls to the stop" is the stop restated as a condition - it says nothing the plan did not.
- Never a hedge. "May weaken" cannot be checked; "falls below" can.
- Standing alone, under the length the schema allows, in one sentence.

## The entitlement test

You are not entitled to argue against a case until you can state its strongest form better than its author did. Write that form first, in one sentence. If the case has no claim worth inverting - the bull case is thin rather than wrong - the honest answer is `neutral`, and manufacturing a bear from generic risks is worse than none: a later stage lowers conviction on it and the record scores the miss.

## Output

The case's strongest form in one sentence; then for each load-bearing claim, the failure, the mechanism and the observable, ranked by damage. The observables are the invalidators.
