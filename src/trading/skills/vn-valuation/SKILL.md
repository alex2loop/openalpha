# VN valuation

Use after quality, never instead of it.

## Anchor on the name's own history

VN multiples are set by domestic liquidity and retail participation as much as by fundamentals. A cross-border comparison to a regional peer imports a different investor base and a different cost of capital. Compare a name to its own five-year range and to its domestic sector first; use a regional comparison only to explain a gap you have already measured.

## Method by sector

- **Banks.** Price to book against sustainable return on equity, then examine asset quality: non-performing loan ratio, group 2 loans, provision coverage, and restructured balances. A low price-to-book on under-provisioned assets is not cheap. CAR, cost/income and NII over average assets per year are in the ANNUAL ACCOUNTS evidence. CAR is measured under Circular 14/2025 (Basel III) from 15 September 2025, so compare a bank's ratio across that date with care. A bank's switch to stock dividends is capital policy, not a warning.
- **Real estate.** Book value is historic cost and understates land; the useful anchor is contracted pre-sales, unbilled backlog, and the funding schedule against it. Legal status of each project is a binary risk, not a discount.
- **Industrials and consumer.** EV/EBITDA and free cash flow yield. Watch working capital: receivable and inventory growth outrunning revenue converts reported profit into a financing need.
- **Utilities and infrastructure.** Regulated returns and the tariff cycle dominate; the multiple follows policy.
- **Cyclicals (shipping, steel, commodities, chemicals).** A low P/E at a freight or price peak is the cycle, not cheapness. Set the P/E against the P/E on average audited annual profit on the value line; a wide gap means the cheapness lasts only as long as the peak does.

## Local accounting caveats

- Revaluation gains, asset disposals, and financial income can carry a whole year's profit growth. Strip them and restate.
- Related-party revenue and receivables need to be identified before margins mean anything.
- Consolidated versus parent-only statements can tell different stories; say which you used.
- Share count changes through bonus issues and stock dividends. A per-share series that is not adjusted for them is wrong.

## Output

The multiple, its position in the name's own range, the sector-appropriate cross-check, and one sentence on what in the accounts would change the number.
