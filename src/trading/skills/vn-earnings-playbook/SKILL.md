# VN earnings playbook

Use when a VN quarterly result is near or has just printed.

## Before the print

The disclosure calendar is public; treat the reporting window as a known event, not a surprise. Do not open a position sized as a normal holding immediately before a result unless the thesis is explicitly about the result - an earnings bet is a different trade with a different risk profile, and it should be sized as one.

## Reading the result

1. **Restate to repeatable earnings.** Remove revaluation gains, asset sales, one-off financial income, and subsidy. What is left is the run rate.
2. **Read the cash flow statement first.** Profit growth with negative operating cash flow is a working-capital story, and it is usually the more important one.
3. **Segment detail.** Group revenue can grow while the core segment shrinks.
4. **Balance sheet delta.** Quarter-on-quarter change in receivables, inventory, and short-term borrowing.
5. **Compare against the same quarter last year and the previous quarter.** VN businesses carry heavy seasonality - construction, retail around Tet, agriculture. A sequential comparison across Tet is meaningless without saying so.

## After the print

- A move on the day is often positioning unwinding rather than the result. Wait for the filing itself before revising a thesis on a headline.
- Revise the thesis explicitly: state what you expected, what happened, and whether the difference is repeatable. A thesis quietly rewritten to fit the new number is untestable.

## What this never produces

An entry, a stop, a target, or a size. It produces a revised view of the business and a statement of whether the original thesis still holds.

## Output

Reported versus restated earnings, the cash flow check, the seasonality caveat, and an explicit thesis-holds or thesis-broken verdict.
