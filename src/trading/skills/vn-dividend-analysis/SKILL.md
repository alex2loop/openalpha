# VN dividend analysis

Use when a VN name is held or judged for its yield, and whenever a dividend is being recorded.

## Is the dividend real

A dividend is a claim on cash, not on reported profit. Check, in order:

1. **Free cash flow cover.** Operating cash flow minus maintenance capital expenditure, against the cash dividend. Cover below one is funded by the balance sheet. Cash dividends paid and free cash flow per year are in the ANNUAL ACCOUNTS evidence. That free cash flow subtracts all capex, so cover against it is a floor.
2. **Where the cash sits.** In a holding-company structure the listed parent may not control subsidiary cash. Upstreaming can be restricted by minority interests, covenants, or regulation.
3. **History.** A single high payout after an asset sale is not a yield. Look for several years of consistent payment.
4. **Payout policy and the AGM resolution.** The announced policy and the approved resolution are the checkable facts; a broker's forecast is not.

A high trailing yield is most often a falling price, not a generous board. Check which moved.

## Cash versus stock dividends

They are different events and the ledger treats them differently:

- A **cash dividend** is income. It is recorded as a monetary event in the payment currency on its payment day.
- A **stock dividend or bonus issue** creates no income at all. It increases share count and mechanically reduces the price per share; the holding's value is unchanged. It is recorded as a quantity adjustment with no currency and no amount.

Treating a stock dividend as income overstates return, and it is the most common recording error in this market.

## Dates that matter

Ex-date, record date, and payment date are distinct. Ownership on the ex-date decides entitlement; the payment day is when cash arrives and is the day the ledger records. Do not record on the announcement.

## Output

Cover ratio with its inputs, the sustainability verdict, and - if the operator is recording - the correct event kind and day.
